# Time as Finality Charter

## Purpose

Determine whether a Lorentz-compatible preferred-frame, or global-tick, theory
can explain physical reality more coherently than the best current account—or
show exactly where it fails.

## Passion

Test whether agents can rigorously investigate a serious heterodox idea:
connect relevant knowledge, separate mechanism from analogy, and try to
falsify it without reflexive dismissal or protection.

## Practice

Develop reusable DJC practice for investigating consequential, unconventional
possibilities and reaching honest, decision-ready conclusions.

## Goals

### 1. Make the Lorentz-compatible global-tick alternative researchable

Define the relevant model family, its commitments, and its serious rivals—rather
than treating “global tick” as a loose intuition.

**Strategies**

- Separate empirically Lorentz-equivalent preferred-frame accounts from models
  that posit observable Lorentz departure or another physical residual.
- For every candidate, state the preferred structure, dynamics, coupling to
  matter or records, relation to observables, and scope.
- Map serious rivals and absorbers before treating a candidate as novel.

**Measures**

- Each live candidate is precise enough to classify as physically
  discriminable, empirically equivalent but explanatory, or undefined and
  withdrawn.
- Each import from another discipline identifies a mechanism, not just an
  analogy.
- Each candidate has a source-backed rival map and a next discriminating
  question.

### 2. Determine whether it earns explanatory and physical standing

Test whether any candidate is compatible with relativity and quantum
constraints, and identify whether it is distinguishable, empirically
equivalent but explanatorily stronger, or untenable.

**Strategies**

- Confront each candidate with the relevant relativistic, quantum,
  no-signalling, cosmological-frame, and known-constraint burdens.
- Seek the cheapest decisive calculation, counterexample, observable, or
  no-go result before expanding the program.
- Compare explanatory gain against added primitives, free parameters, and
  repair assumptions.

**Measures**

- Every live candidate has either a credible falsifier or an explicit
  interpretation-only status.
- A conclusion names what is forced, what remains open, and the strongest
  surviving rival.
- No claimed gain depends on an undeclared repair, boundary move, or expanded
  state space.

### 3. Establish what it contributes to time, records, and finality

Determine whether a preferred-frame account explains those phenomena better
than causal-partial-order accounts, and isolate any independent result or
constraint that Dynamic Unity can use without absorbing Time as Finality’s
research role.

**Strategies**

- Compare global-update and causal-partial-order accounts on record formation,
  irreversibility, finality, and observer access.
- Preserve both positive and negative results as possible independent
  scientific contributions.
- Route any Dynamic Unity-relevant result by pointer, preserving each owner's
  research truth and avoiding a second integrative theory.

**Measures**

- Each comparison states exactly what the preferred-frame model changes, and
  what it leaves unchanged.
- Each completed line returns an independent result, constraint, or explicit
  non-contribution—not a restatement of Dynamic Unity work.
- Cross-repository use names the source, boundary, and receiving-owner
  disposition without copying owner truth.

## Research guardrails

- A global tick is a model family, not a conclusion.
- Empirical equivalence, an interpretation-only result, or a precise failure
  is a successful outcome.
- The repository does not use cross-disciplinary resemblance as evidence;
  a proposed bridge must identify a mechanism and a testable burden.
- Dynamic Unity integrates the broader geometry-and-record picture. Time as
  Finality independently researches this alternative and returns only
  source-bounded results or constraints.
